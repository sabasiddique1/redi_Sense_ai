section: Community-acquired pneumonia

Clinical concern for pneumonia is stronger when cough, fever, dyspnea, pleuritic chest pain,
or new focal lung findings occur together. Chest imaging is commonly used to confirm a
suspected pulmonary infiltrate or consolidation when the clinical picture suggests infection.

Escalation decisions should reflect illness severity, oxygenation, comorbidity burden, and
hemodynamic stability. Patients with hypoxemia, confusion, rapidly progressive respiratory
symptoms, or sepsis features need urgent in-person evaluation rather than routine follow-up.
